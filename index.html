The first decision is the job definition, not the door handle style. Separate a visible-refresh update from a strip-out rebuild, because each path changes trades, permits, sequencing and the value of an itemised fixed-price quote. A kitchen can keep its footprint while replacing doors, benchtops and splashback, or it can be replanned so services, storage, lighting and appliance clearances move before manufacture. Before anyone prices the work, record the existing layout, access limits, appliance positions and anything worth retaining. Photograph corners, note where plumbing and power enter, list pain points such as poor bench runs or missing pantry space, and decide whether character must be preserved in a period home or modernised in a newer rebuild. A shortlist scorecard that keeps quotes honest

A comparison sheet should make assumptions visible. Score every proposal on the same basis, then interview the gaps rather than the sales line. A strong quote should show what was measured on site, what has been assumed, and which items are provisional. For a refresh, confirm which existing elements remain in place. For a rebuild, confirm the order of strip-out, rough-in, cabinetry, benchtop templating and finishing. For custom joinery, ask how drawers, butler's pantry storage and cabinet depths are specified before materials are ordered.

  • Measured scope: what was taped on site versus guessed from photos.
  • Fixed price: whether demolition, rough-in, cabinetry, benchtops, splashback, appliances and finishing are itemised.
  • Variation method: how drawing changes before manufacture are priced and approved.
  • Joinery detail: drawer sizes, butler's pantry storage, cabinet depths and hardware.
  • Program: stated lead times for cabinetry and stone, then on-site work.
  • Documents: permits, registered plumbing and electrical trades, warranties and handover paperwork.

Where two quotes look close, the difference is often hidden in allowances, exclusions or the point at which cabinetry manufacture begins. Ask for the assumption log to be written down, not explained over the phone.

Compliance checks before deposit or signature

Victorian rules should be treated as a signing gate, not a footnote. The sourced page states a written contract is required for a major domestic building contract over $10,000 under the Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995. It also notes domestic building insurance is required above the current threshold, commonly cited as $16,000, and says the live figure should be checked with the VBA. Confirm which permits apply to the exact scope, who obtains them, which regulated work is by licensed or registered trades, and what certificates and warranties arrive at completion.

Keep the trail practical. Request the contract name, builder registration details where relevant, insurance certificate requirements for the contract value, permit responsibility, and a list of regulated plumbing and electrical tasks. If drawings change after signing, record the variation in writing with cost and time impact before cabinets are made. At handover, collect compliance paperwork, warranty information and final invoices so the project file matches the promises made before work began.

Benchtop and material choices after the 2024 silica rule

Material selection is now a compliance question as much as an aesthetic one. From 1 July 2024, engineered stone containing 1% or more crystalline silica was banned for manufacture, supply, processing and installation across Australia under model WHS Regulations. The page lists low-silica options such as porcelain, sintered stone, natural granite or marble, solid timber, stainless steel, laminate, and engineered stone under 1% crystalline silica. The useful question is not only which surface looks best, but which one suits the way the room will be used and the cabinetry plan already chosen.

Ask what can be templated for the existing plan, which edges and joins are available, how seams will appear on long runs, and whether the surface tolerates heat, staining and cleaning habits in a busy household. A lower-cost refresh may keep the layout while changing visible finishes; a full rebuild can align surface selection with new storage, lighting and appliance clearances. Record the selected material, supplier, edge detail and any care notes in the same file as the quote and drawings.
